# Finken — MASTER PRODUCT CONTEXT

## Instructions for AI

This document is the source of truth for the Finken product.

When working on Finken:
- Preserve established product decisions unless explicitly asked to revisit them.
- Do not assume a feature belongs in V1 simply because it is mentioned as a future possibility.
- Distinguish between current decisions, proposed ideas, and future roadmap items.
- Keep Finken's core philosophy intact: "Simple on the surface. Intelligent underneath."
- Prefer simple UX over accounting/ERP complexity.
- When proposing new features, explain how they fit into the existing Finken architecture.
- Do not unnecessarily reinvent decisions already documented here.
- If a new proposal conflicts with an existing decision, explicitly identify the conflict before suggesting a change.

## Current Working Decisions

- Product name: Finken
- Tagline direction: "Your Money, Made Simple."
- Category: Personal & Small Business Financial Management
- Core philosophy: Capture → Organize → Understand → Plan → Act → Improve
- Core architecture: Shared Money Engine with Personal and Business contexts
- User-facing organizational concept: Spaces
- Product philosophy: Simple on the surface. Intelligent underneath.

# Finken

### Your Money, Made Simple.

## 1. Product Vision

**Finken is a simple, intelligent personal and small-business money management platform that helps people capture, organize, understand, plan, and act on their finances.**

Finken is not intended to feel like accounting software.

It should feel like a **friendly financial companion** that quietly helps users answer:

> **Where is my money?**\
> **Where is it going?**\
> **What's coming up?**\
> **What can I afford?**\
> **What should I do next?**

The core product philosophy is:

### **Capture → Organize → Understand → Plan → Act → Improve**

---

# 2. The Product Opportunity

Most expense applications focus primarily on:

> **"Record what you spent."**

Finken should go further:

> **"Record your money once, and let Finken help you understand and manage it."**

The product therefore has three layers:

### Layer 1 — Money Management

Record and organize financial activity.

### Layer 2 — Financial Intelligence

Understand spending, income, obligations, trends and behaviour.

### Layer 3 — Financial Action

Budgets, goals, reminders, recommendations and financial decisions.

This creates a product that can start simple but grow into a broader financial platform.

## **Spaces**

A Space is a financial environment.

A user could have:

🏠 **Personal**

💼 **Business**

👨‍👩‍👧 **Family**

🚗 **Car**

🏢 **Company A**

🛍 **Side Business**

Each Space has its own:

- Income
- Expenses
- Categories
- Budgets
- Goals
- Receivables
- Payables
- Loans
- Dashboard
- Analytics

Users can switch between Spaces without creating separate Finken accounts.

This allows Finken to remain simple for an individual while also supporting increasingly sophisticated users.

# 6. Quick Capture — The Heart of Finken

The most important UX principle:

> **Recording money must be faster than forgetting about it.**

The primary action should be:

### `+ Add`

The user chooses:

**Expense | Income | Transfer | Receive/Pay | Loan**

For example:

> Rs. 850

🍔 Food → Dining

🏠 Personal

Today

"Family dinner"

**Save**

The goal is a transaction taking only a few seconds.

---

# 7. Intelligent Capture

As Finken learns the user's behaviour, it should reduce input further.

If someone repeatedly records:

> Rs. 500 → Lunch → Food

Finken can suggest:

> **Lunch — Rs. 500?**

The longer-term vision is natural-language capture:

> "Spent 850 on dinner"

Finken interprets:

**Rs. 850 → Food → Dining → Personal**

The user confirms and saves.

This can eventually become one of the strongest differentiators of Finken.

# 19. Behavioural Analytics

This is where Finken moves beyond an expense tracker.

Instead of:

> Food = Rs. 42,000

Finken identifies:

> Food spending increased 23% this month.

Then:

> Dining accounts for 41% of your Food spending.

Then:

> You usually spend more on dining during weekends.

Analytics can identify:

- Spending trends
- Category changes
- Spending frequency
- Weekend behaviour
- Recurring patterns
- Budget adherence
- Income trends
- Cash-flow patterns
- Unusual transactions
- Expense concentration

The language should remain neutral and useful.

Not:

> "You waste too much money."

Instead:

> "Dining is currently your fastest-growing expense category."

---

# 20. Money Health

Finken can eventually provide a lightweight financial-health indicator.

Not a credit score.

Not a judgment.

Instead:

## Money Health

**Improving**

Based on:

- Budget consistency
- Savings progress
- Upcoming obligations
- Spending volatility
- Receivable/payable position

Finken explains *why* the indicator changed.

> Budget consistency ↑\
> Savings consistency ↑\
> Spending volatility ↓

The emphasis is on improvement rather than judgment.

---

# 21. Finken Insights

The original "Financial Literacy Digest" becomes something more actionable:

# **Finken Insights**

> **"What should I know or do next?"**

Examples:

### Spending Insight

> Dining increased 27% this month.

`Review Dining`

### Reminder

> Ali owes you Rs. 8,000.

`Remind Ali`

### Budget Alert

> You're likely to exceed your Shopping budget.

`Review Budget`

### Goal Insight

> You have Rs. 15,000 above your savings target.

`Review Goal`

The content should be personalized and action-driven rather than generic financial articles.

# 22. Financial Literacy

Finken can still have an educational layer, but keep it contextual.

Instead of a long educational course:

> 💡 **Did you know?**

When a user creates a budget:

> A budget works best when you review spending throughout the month rather than only at month-end.

When creating a loan:

> 💡 Finken can remind you before your repayment is due.

Education becomes part of the product rather than a separate content library.

The system should return answers from the user's own financial data.

# 39. What Finken Should NOT Become Initially

Avoid turning Finken into an ERP.

Don't initially overload it with:

- Inventory
- Payroll
- Complex taxation
- Full accounting workflows
- Complex double-entry UI
- Large accounting reports
- Investment management
- Cryptocurrency
- Excessive banking integrations
- Hundreds of categories
- Complicated permissions
- AI features added only for novelty

The backend can be designed for future expansion.

The front-end should remain simple.

# 42. The Ultimate Finken Positioning

Finken should not compete only in the category:

> **Expense Tracker**

Its broader category is:

> **Personal & Small Business Financial Management**

And its long-term positioning can be:

# **Finken**

### **Know your money. Manage your money.**

The product promise:

> **Finken brings your income, expenses, obligations, goals and financial behaviour into one simple place — then helps you understand what is happening and decide what to do next.**

The product should always feel like:

### **Simple on the surface. Intelligent underneath.**

That, in my view, is the strongest version of the idea: **a very easy expense/income recorder at the entry point, a financial-management tool as users mature, and eventually a monetizable financial platform underneath.**
